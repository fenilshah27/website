Why I write

Writing is where I explore ideas before they become institutions.

Where it begins

Many of the organisations, initiatives, and projects I have built began as questions, observations, or reflections captured through writing. It allows me to examine complex issues, challenge assumptions, and develop ideas that contribute to education, research, governance, and society.

What it explores

My writing focuses on the systems that shape our world and the institutions that sustain it. While some pieces are analytical and research driven, others are reflective, exploring leadership, human behaviour, public policy, and the future of knowledge.

What it should do

I believe that writing should not only communicate ideas but also encourage thoughtful dialogue, inspire action, and contribute to a broader understanding of the challenges and opportunities facing society.

Areas of writing

Five subjects.

Analytical, research driven, and reflective pieces across the areas that shape how institutions and society evolve.

01

Institutions and Governance

How institutions are established, governed, and sustained across generations, and the principles that enable them to create lasting public value.

02

Education

The future of education, assessment, learning systems, academic culture, and institutional innovation.

03

Research

Research ecosystems, academic publishing, scholarly communication, and the role of knowledge in addressing societal challenges.

04

Leadership

Leadership as stewardship, organisational culture, ethical decision making, and the responsibility of building institutions that outlast their founders.

05

Technology and Society

How technological change influences governance, education, research, public policy, and human behaviour.
